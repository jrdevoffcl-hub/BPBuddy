Understanding hypertension

Why blood pressure management needs consistent follow-up

Hypertension is a major health concern in the Philippines. It increases the risk of heart attack, stroke, and premature death, which makes regular blood pressure monitoring and proper management important.

Patients may need to record their readings, take prescribed medicines consistently, attend scheduled check-ups, and keep track of symptoms and lifestyle habits. In rural and underserved communities, these tasks can become harder when records are paper-based, follow-ups are irregular, or internet access is limited.

BPBuddy supports organization and follow-up. It does not diagnose hypertension, prescribe medicine, or replace a physician or other health professional.

How BPBuddy works

One connected workflow for two user roles

  1. 1
    Create an account

    Register as a patient or barangay health worker. Each role gets a dashboard designed for its responsibilities.

  2. 2
    Record and review

    Patients add self-monitoring information. BPBuddy validates the form, keeps records organized, and shows BP trends.

  3. 3
    Synchronize records

    Entries are saved locally first. Queued records become available in the BHW view after synchronization.

  4. 4
    Coordinate follow-up

    BHWs identify patients needing attention, save follow-up dates, add notes, and prepare structured referral summaries.

Why BPBuddy

Designed around documented gaps in hypertension management

The project documentation identifies four connected challenges worth addressing: irregular BP monitoring, weak follow-up, fragmented referral information, and limited connectivity in rural or underserved settings.

BPBuddy combines patient self-monitoring and community follow-up within one web-based workflow so information can be organized around the same patient record.

National context

WHO reports approximately 16.8 million Filipino adults with hypertension, or about one in three.

Follow-up gap

A Philippine low-income study reported that only 48% received a follow-up visit after diagnosis and 17% discontinued treatment at some point.

Digital opportunity

Related studies in the project literature found that web-based, mobile, telehealth, and community health worker interventions can support monitoring, adherence, and linkage to care.

Evidence summary follows the BPBuddy project documentation, including WHO Western Pacific (2025) [1] and the cited Philippine hypertension-control study [2].

Frequently asked questions

Before you use BPBuddy

Does BPBuddy diagnose hypertension?

No. BPBuddy organizes self-monitoring information and provides educational warnings. Diagnosis and treatment decisions belong to qualified health professionals.

Can I enter a reading without internet?

Yes. Patient entries are stored on the device first. If the browser is offline, the record remains queued and can synchronize when connectivity becomes available.

What can a BHW see?

A BHW account can view patient profiles and records that have been synchronized in this browser-based implementation, then organize follow-ups, notes, and referrals.

Is BPBuddy for other diseases?

No. The current project scope is limited to hypertension self-monitoring and community follow-up.
